## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Category-interchangeable. The homepage is credible, restrained, and coherent, but it still reads as a polished résumé template. Another senior AI professional could replace the portrait, biography, career history, and publication list without changing the composition. For a professional site, Alejandro's positioning and evidence of impact should lead; here, the portrait is the dominant artifact while the differentiating story is dispersed across Experience, Education, News, and Publications. The interface does not communicate at a glance what Alejandro uniquely does, the kind of problems he solves, or the professional impact visitors should remember.

## Overall Impression

The site creates trust quickly and has a disciplined visual foundation. Its biggest opportunity is to turn a résumé-shaped page into a memorable professional profile: who Alejandro is, what he uniquely contributes, and evidence that supports it. The current emotional peak is the portrait; the natural peak should be a concise impact narrative backed by selected proof.

## What's Working

1. **Fast credibility formation:** The current role, employers, institutions, dates, and publication venues form a convincing professional record.
2. **Disciplined visual system:** Plus Jakarta Sans, restrained teal accents, charcoal surfaces, dividers, spacing, and focus styling create a cohesive foundation.
3. **Useful technical depth:** Publication cards provide direct paths to papers, code, posters, slides, DOI/arXiv records, and bibliography data for visitors who want deeper evidence.

## Cognitive Load and Emotional Journey

The page fails 5 of 8 cognitive-load checks: single focus, chunking, visual hierarchy, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. Decision points exceeding four visible choices include both five-link social clusters, desktop navigation, eight News updates, five selected publications, and publication cards with five to six resource controls.

The entry feels calm, credible, and professional. Orientation weakens because there is no concise professional thesis or impact summary telling visitors what to remember. Energy falls through a résumé-like middle, and deeper proof is spread between experience entries and publications that begin around 2,021px on desktop and 2,862px on mobile. The page ends with copyright rather than a clear invitation to review the CV, contact Alejandro, or explore selected evidence.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Professional positioning and impact are buried inside the résumé

**Why it matters:** The primary visitor goal is to understand Alejandro's professional profile and impact. The current role is clear, but the unique value proposition and supporting outcomes require visitors to infer a story from chronology, biography, and publications.

**Fix:** Rewrite the first screen around a concise professional value proposition using only verifiable existing facts. Follow it with two or three impact pillars or selected proof points drawn from current roles, projects, and publications. Keep the full chronology lower on the page or in the CV, and provide one clear next action such as "View CV" or "Explore selected work."

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] Mobile hierarchy and target sizing obstruct the primary path

**Why it matters:** At 390px, the portrait occupies roughly 360 by 360px, publications begin near 2,862px, and the page is about 6,003px tall. Social, menu, and publication controls measure roughly 20-36px high, below the 44px touch-target guideline.

**Fix:** Use a 112-144px portrait/media-object layout on mobile, put the role and professional value proposition before or beside it, enlarge controls to 44px, and reduce each publication to one primary action plus a "More resources" disclosure.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Redundant choices dilute intent

**Why it matters:** Five social links appear twice, CV is available through multiple routes, and each publication presents an unprioritized matrix of links.

**Fix:** Keep one labeled contact cluster. Choose one publication action such as "Read paper" or "View project" as primary, move metadata into secondary disclosure, and show three News items followed by "All updates."

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`

### [P2] The visual language does not express the professional identity

**Why it matters:** The page could represent almost any senior technologist. It contains no authored motif or evidence pattern that connects Alejandro's identity to applied AI, coding agents, evaluation, optimization, or technical leadership.

**Fix:** Build a distinctive but professional evidence language from existing material: compact impact metrics, evaluation matrices, repository or code-change diagrams, convergence curves, and concise project summaries. Use them as proof, not decoration.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder`

### [P2] The ending wastes accumulated credibility

**Why it matters:** After a long evidence list, the experience ends administratively instead of turning professional interest into a next step.

**Fix:** Add a concise closing block with the current professional focus, the kinds of problems Alejandro works on, an email action, the CV, and one recommended proof point.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer):** Encounters five unlabeled social glyphs before a primary action, finds key routes behind the mobile menu, receives no plain-language summary of Alejandro's professional impact, and must assemble the value proposition from résumé entries and technical terminology such as coverage, token cost, DOI, arXiv, and BibTeX.

**Riley (Deliberate Stress Tester):** Finds few linked outcomes or case studies supporting experience claims, sees inconsistent resource sets across publication cards, encounters nested News scrolling without a strong continuation cue, and discovers redundant social/CV paths rather than a stable information architecture.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):** Loses most of the first viewport to the portrait, faces an approximately 6,003px page, encounters undersized controls, must decode icon-only social actions, and can accidentally compete with the nested News scroller while trying to move down the page.

## Minor Observations

- Justified biography text creates uneven spacing; left alignment would improve reading rhythm.
- Experience summaries run too wide on desktop.
- The portrait alternative text and global focus-visible styling are solid accessibility foundations.
- Metadata contrast can become overly recessive.
- Venue colors differentiate publications but do not explain why each work matters.
- Uniform section dividers make every section feel equally important.
